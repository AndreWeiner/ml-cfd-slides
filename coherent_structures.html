Lecture 6 review

What makes a flow structure coherent?
Its spatially correlated features evolve together in an organized way. A recurring structure can retain a consistent phase relationship between different locations.
Why look for coherent structures in a complex flow?
They can explain a quantity of interest, such as fluctuating lift, and help identify where to measure or apply control.
What sustains transonic shock buffet on an airfoil?
Feedback between the shock and the boundary layer sustains shock motion and changes in downstream separation.
How can aerodynamic buffet lead to structural buffeting?
Unsteady aerodynamic loads can excite structural modes and produce a strong aeroelastic response.
What does the measured cylinder-wake dataset contain?
Planar PIV snapshots of the streamwise and transverse velocity components on a fixed grid. Masked regions are excluded from the analysis.
How do we obtain a velocity fluctuation at a probe?
Subtract the temporal sample mean at that probe from each instantaneous measurement.
What does negative covariance between the two wake probes tell us?
When one probe is above its mean, the other tends to be below its mean. The paired scatter is elongated along a direction with opposite signs.
How are the snapshots organized in the data matrix?
Each column is one time snapshot. Each row represents a spatial degree of freedom, such as a probe or a velocity component at a grid point.
What does the first principal axis maximize?
The sample variance of the projected, centered data among all unit directions.
How do we identify a principal axis from the covariance matrix?
The stationarity condition gives a covariance eigenproblem. The first axis is an eigenvector for the largest eigenvalue, which equals the variance along that axis.
How do we organize both measured velocity components for full-field POD?
Stack the flattened streamwise and transverse fields in each snapshot column. Split the spatial modes at the same index to plot both components.
What is the maximum rank of a centered snapshot matrix with N snapshots?
At most N − 1, because the centered snapshot columns sum to zero. The rank is also bounded by the number of spatial degrees of freedom.
What links the economy SVD to the spatial POD of centered data?
The left singular vectors give the spatial modes. Each covariance eigenvalue equals the squared singular value divided by N − 1.
Why is projecting onto the economy basis and back exact for the original snapshots?
The retained nonzero left singular vectors span the snapshot column space. The projection preserves any vector in that span.
What do the POD time coefficients tell us?
They are the coordinates of each snapshot in the spatial POD basis. Their time histories show how the modal amplitudes and signs evolve.
Why use the method of snapshots when there are many more spatial points than snapshots?
It solves the eigenproblem in an N × N Gram matrix. Nonzero temporal eigenvectors can then be mapped back to spatial modes.
What changes when we keep the mean before taking the SVD?
The decomposition analyzes uncentered second moments. A strong mean can dominate the first mode. Exact alignment also requires the mean direction to be an eigenvector of the centered covariance and to remain leading.
Why can we compute the DFT on POD time coefficients?
The DFT acts along time and the POD basis acts along space. With all nonzero POD modes, transforming the coefficients and projecting back recovers the same Fourier field.
What does the phase of a complex Fourier mode tell us?
Relative phase across locations shows how the single-frequency pattern progresses in time. Animating one period helps distinguish apparent propagation from nearly simultaneous motion.
What does a Strouhal number near 0.194 represent in this dataset?
It is the dominant dimensionless shedding frequency, with St = fD/U∞. A harmonic completes an integer number of cycles during one period of the fundamental.
